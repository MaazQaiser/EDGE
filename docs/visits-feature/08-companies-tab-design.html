FilterGo · Visits · Design spec

A company, its locations, and a year of visits in twelve columns

The Companies tab, specified against the client call rather than inferred from it. Three things that call settled and 07 did not have: what a location is and that it is never a company, the parent–child company relationship the whole view hangs on, and multi-select across companies because planning happens across several at once. The year becomes a month matrix — one row per location, twelve columns, the dates in the cells.

The call

What was settled, and what was newly demanded

07 was written from a second-hand account of the rejection. The call notes confirm most of it and add three requirements that change the shape of the thing. Worth separating the two, because the confirmations mean 07's decisions can stand and the new demands mean some of them cannot.

Confirmed — 07 was right about these

  • A list, not a calendar. Emphatic: for a quarterly customer a calendar means clicking next twelve times. “That's not the regular customer experience.”
  • Dates are information text, not links. Asked directly whether clicking Sept 8 should jump to Sept 8 — the answer was no. 07 D20 holds, with one refinement the matrix forces (D33).
  • Company names on the left, visits to the right. The layout in one phrase.
  • Two ways to look at the same view — a time-scoped multi-company list, and a single-company forward year. Not two screens.
  • Volume falls out of cadence: next 4 visits if quarterly, next 12 if monthly. 07 D18 got this right.
  • The current build showing only this week — “Not helpful. Not helpful at all. This doesn't work.”

New — and each one changes the design

  • Site = property = location. Synonyms for one thing: a physical address. That definition has to persist identically across Leads, SET and Edge. This is a vocabulary requirement with teeth — see the vocabulary.
  • Company ≠ site, and the parent–child company relationship must be built. “Mega Saver” the parent versus all the Mega Saver locations in a franchisee's territory. 07 assumed a flat customerId; that is not enough.
  • Multi-select across companies, because FilterGo plans across several at once — then filter down to a single location. 07's rail selected exactly one company and used that selection as the mode switch. That has to go (D31).
Read this as a warning, not a footnote The notes record that he will hold Mehroz accountable if this comes up again — and the “this” is the company-versus-site conflation, not a feature. So the vocabulary section below is the most load-bearing part of this document, and it is deliberately first. A screen that gets the layout right and the nouns wrong fails the same way the week calendar did.
Vocabulary

One physical address, three words, and a company is none of them

The instruction is that site, property and location denote the same thing — a physical address — and that the definition must be stable across three products. The design consequence is not which word wins; it is that only one of them may appear on a screen, and that the word for the address is never used for the company that occupies it.

TermIsIs not
Company parent A trading name that occupies many addresses. Mega Saver. The row group. Not an address. Not a billing account. Not one shop.
Account The billing entity a visit is invoiced to — customerId, already the key invoicing uses. Not the thing the franchisee names when they talk about a customer.
Location = site = property One physical address that gets serviced. The row. Never a company, never a contract, never a route.
Visit One service occurrence at one location on one date. API: hit. Not a runsheet, which is a day's route of many visits at many locations.

Edge's data layer already says site everywhere — siteId, the Sites module, VISIT_SITE_BOOK. Renaming that is a migration nobody asked for. But the displayed noun already goes through getLabel('terms', 'sites', t), which means the word on screen is a tenant configuration change, not a code change. So: keep site as the data key, set the display term once per tenant, and never hardcode any of the three in copy. If the client wants FilterGo to read Locations, that is one line in the tenant labels payload and a TENANT_LABELS_VERSION bump (handoff §7.2).

Why this is cheap here and expensive later Every noun on this screen is already a labelled term in this codebase except one: company. The data says customerId, company and client in three different files — sites.mock.js uses company, invoice.mock.js uses client. One term has to win and it should go through getLabel like every other tenant noun, before a second screen learns the wrong one.
The model

Parent, account, location — and why all three have to exist

“Mega Saver the parent versus all the Mega Saver locations” is a request for a level that does not exist in the data today. Each Mega Saver shop is very likely its own customer record with its own customerId, because that is how it is invoiced — and nothing links them, which is exactly why the franchisee cannot ask “when am I next at a Mega Saver”.

So the model is three levels, and the reason it is three rather than two is that billing and recognition disagree: billing needs the account, the franchisee recognises the brand, and the technician drives to the address.

Two levels on screen, three in the data

Rendering all three tiers would triple the row count to answer a question nobody on this screen is asking. The franchisee wants which Mega Savers am I visiting, and when — not which legal entity signs the cheque. So the account appears as a small badge on a location row, and only where a company has more than one; otherwise it is silent.

Territory is the trap Mega Saver nationally might be four hundred locations. This franchisee has six. If the company header reads 6 locations that is correct and useful; if it reads 400 locations and lists six, it is a support ticket. Every count on this screen is the count of what is shown, and the header says so in words — 6 locations in your territory. 07 had this as a one-line edge case; the parent–child model promotes it to a decision (D27), because a parent is exactly the kind of object that spans territories.
Structure

One tab, two modes, and a rail that filters instead of navigating

“Two ways to look at the same view.” Both modes are projections of one visit set, so they cannot disagree, and the switch between them is explicit rather than a side effect of selecting something.

ModeAnswersShape
Period default “Who am I visiting today / this week / this month?” Scope control, then companies → the locations being visited → the dates. Overdue pinned above. Substantially 07's View A.
Year “When is this customer due, all year?” A 12-month matrix: one row per location, twelve month columns, day-of-month numbers in the cells. Replaces 07's stacked date list.

The rail is a filter now, not a mode switch

07 D15 made selecting a company the switch from the roll-up to the year. Multi-select breaks that: selecting four companies cannot mean “show me one company's year”. So the rail becomes a multi-select list — none selected means all — and it narrows whichever mode you are in. A location filter then narrows within the selection, which is the “consolidate at company level, then filter down to a single location” requirement taken literally (D31).

This is strictly more capable than the old behaviour and it costs nothing: selecting exactly one company and switching to Year is the single-company year the client asked for, and selecting six gives the cross-company planning view that 07 could not express at all.

The year view

Twelve columns, and the rhythm becomes visible

A stacked list of dates per location tells you when. A month matrix tells you when and shows you the shape of the arrangement — a monthly location is a solid line of twelve, a quarterly one is four marks with three gaps between them, and an unscheduled one is a conspicuously empty row. That is the Subscribe & Save trick made spatial, and it is the reason to prefer the grid.

It is only drawable because the arithmetic is small. Across the demo book's cadences the busiest interval — fourteen days — puts at most three visits in a month, and a monthly location puts exactly one in each. So a cell holds one to three numbers and never needs a “+2 more”.

What a cell contains

Cell stateRendering
One to three visitsThe day-of-month numbers, middle-dot separated, tabular figures so columns align. No month name — the column is the month.
Four or more bounded4 dates plus a popover on the cell. No cadence in the current book reaches this; a weekly contract would, and it must not blow the row height.
EmptyNothing. Not a dash, not a zero — an empty cell in a rhythm is information and punctuation would clutter it.
Already completedGreen, the same green a completed visit is drawn in everywhere else. Only occurs in the current month, which is the one column a rolling year opens in the middle of.
Mixed kinds in one cellCommon, not exceptional: the current month mixes completed with planned, and the horizon month mixes planned with projected. Each date keeps its own treatment.
Overdue / missedAlert colour and the word, in the month it belongs to. A missed visit outlives its date (06 D5) so it cannot be silently dropped from a customer-facing grid.
No cadence at allThe row spans all twelve columns with amber Not scheduled and the last visit date. Sorted to the top of its company block (D34).

Geometry

  • The location column is sticky, because a row read at the June column is meaningless without its name. Month headers stick to the top for the same reason.
  • Twelve columns at 46px plus a 212px label column is about 1030px — it fits the scheduler pane on a desktop and scrolls horizontally inside its own container on anything narrower. The page never scrolls sideways.
  • ~34px rows. 18 companies and 46 locations is 64 rows, which scrolls in one pane without virtualization; the pane virtualizes when the book is larger.
  • Company blocks collapse, and default to expanded — the point of the screen is seeing them at once.
  • A /yr total column, right-aligned, because the row already contains that arithmetic and it is the number a stock order is built from (D35).
The honesty rule

The horizon is a column boundary, and it lands mid-month

07's planned/projected distinction survives intact and is still the load-bearing idea: visits are real objects only inside the window the scheduler has actually generated, and beyond it there is a cadence, an anchor and a multiplication. A date that reads as firm and then moves costs more than one that was labelled an estimate.

What the matrix changes is where the boundary is drawn. In a stacked list it is a horizontal rule partway down a location's dates. In a grid it is vertical — and because the scheduler's horizon is a date, not a month, it falls inside a column rather than between two. Three rules follow, and the first one is easy to get wrong:

  • One rule for the whole grid, drawn at the column containing scheduledHorizonEndsAt — not at each row's own first projection. A quarterly location whose next visit is in November has nothing planned in September, but the horizon is still September; deriving the rule per row would draw a different boundary on every line and stop meaning anything.
  • The horizon column is not washed, because it holds both kinds. Every column after it is, because every date in it is arithmetic. The wash is the summary; the cell is the truth.
  • Inside that straddling column, each date carries its own weight — full ink for planned, lighter for projected.

The first column holds a third kind of date

A rolling twelve months starts at the current month, so column one contains visits that have already happened — Downtown Plaza was serviced on the 3rd and is due again on the 17th and the 31st. That is not planned and it is certainly not projected, so the honesty rule has three states in that column, not two, and one of them is history.

Rather than invent a treatment for it, the matrix borrows the scheduler's own status vocabulary — completed reads green, missed reads alert, exactly as a visit card does — so helper/visitState.js stays the single source of truth for what a visit is and no second palette has to be kept in step with it. Projection is the only thing this surface adds, and it is the only thing drawn in a way the grid has no equivalent for.

The failure this avoids Drawing the boundary at the nearest month edge would be tidier and would lie in one of two directions: round it forward and three planned visits get labelled projections; round it back and projections get read as commitments. The straddling column is slightly less elegant and exactly right, which is the same trade 06 D11 made when it let a resolved state overrule a stored one.
Interaction

What responds, and what is only ink

The requirement is that dates do not navigate. The matrix strains it in one place — a row now spans a year and many visits, so “the row is the click target” no longer identifies anything. The target moves down a level to the cell, and the date keeps its inertness.

TargetBehaviour
A date numberInert. No link styling, no underline, no cursor change, on either kind of date. Unchanged from D20.
A cell holding planned visitsHover wash on the cell; click opens a small popover listing that month's visits with window, route and status — each opening the existing visit drawer, with 06's state rules (D3/D4/D5) already governing what it offers.
A cell holding only projectionsInert, with a tooltip stating provenance: projected from a 90-day cadence · not yet scheduled.
A location nameOpens the location, on its Schedule tab. Names are navigation; dates are not.
A company headerCollapses and expands its block. The name itself opens the company record where one exists.
The account badgeInert. It is a disambiguator, not a route to billing — this is a read surface.
The Unassigned pillThe one write action on the tab, as in 07 D21. Opens the same add-to-a-route flow the grid uses.
ExportCSV of the visible grid, months as columns. Download only — handing a schedule to a customer is the franchisee's conversation.
Everything elseNot here. Bulk selection, harmonize and route building stay on the Visits tab.
Decisions

D25 – D36

Numbering continues 06 and 07, so the visits record stays one list. Rows marked in the alert colour change or reverse an earlier decision.

ID
Decision
Why
D25
Site, property and location are one thing — a physical address. One displayed noun, supplied by getLabel; the other two never appear beside it.
Stated requirement, and it must hold across Leads, SET and Edge. Keeping site as the data key and the noun as a tenant label makes it a config change rather than a migration.
D26
Three levels in the data — company (parent) → account → location — and two on screen. The account is a badge on a location row, shown only when a company has more than one.
Billing needs the account, the franchisee recognises the brand, the technician drives to the address. A third visible tier triples the rows to answer a question this screen is not asking.
D27
Every row and every count is scoped to the user's territory, and the company header says so in words.
A parent company is exactly the object that spans territories. “400 locations” over six rows is a support ticket; 07 had this as an edge case, the parent model makes it structural.
D28
The year view is a 12-month matrix: locations are rows, months are columns, cells carry day-of-month numbers. Replaces 07's stacked per-location date list.
It answers “when” and additionally shows the shape of the arrangement, which is what makes a quarterly customer legible at a glance. Viable because the busiest cadence in the book puts only three visits in a month.
D29
Twelve months rolling from the current month, not a calendar year. No year stepper.
“Forward-looking 12 months.” The first column is then always the one being asked about, and December does not turn half the grid into history.
D30
The planned/projected boundary is one vertical dashed rule for the whole grid, at the column containing scheduledHorizonEndsAt — never derived per row. That column stays unwashed and its dates carry individual weight; every later column is washed.
The horizon is a date, so it falls inside a column. Rounding it to a month edge would either demote planned visits or promote guesses; deriving it per row would draw a different line on every line and mean nothing. Same principle as 06 D11.
D31
Company selection is multi-select and acts as a filter. None selected means all. A location filter narrows within the selection. Reverses 07 D15, where selecting one company was the mode switch.
“Multi-select across companies, since the use case is planning across several at once.” One selection cannot both mean a filter and a navigation. Selecting exactly one still gives the single-company year.
D32
Two modes on one tab — Period (today / this week / this month) and Year — switched explicitly. Period is the default.
“Two ways to look at the same view.” Both are projections of one visit set, so nothing can disagree. Period is the default because it is the shift-to-shift question.
D33
Dates stay inert; the cell is the click target, opening a popover of that month's visits. Refines D20, which made the row the target.
A row spans twelve months now, so it identifies nothing. Confirmed on the call: clicking Sept 8 must not jump to Sept 8. A cell is a month of one location — small enough to be an unambiguous subject.
D34
A location with no cadence spans all twelve columns as amber Not scheduled with its last visit date, sorted to the top of its company block.
Same treatment as the grid (06 D8) so the fact reads identically in both places. An empty row in a rhythm grid would otherwise be indistinguishable from a loading failure.
D35
A /yr total column at the right edge. Retires 07 D18's twelve-row cap, which the matrix makes unnecessary.
The row already contains the arithmetic, and annual volume is what a filter stock order is built from. The cap existed to stop a weekly location producing 52 rows — in a grid it produces four numbers in a cell instead.
D36
A date's treatment comes from helper/visitState.js — completed green, missed alert, the same as a visit card. Projected is the only vocabulary this surface adds. The current month therefore holds three kinds of date, not two.
A rolling year opens in the middle of a month, so history is unavoidable in column one. Borrowing the resolver means a visit cannot read one way on the grid and another here — the defect 06 D6 was written to end — and only one new mark has to be learned.
Edge cases

What breaks a grid that a list did not have to answer

CaseBehaviour
One brand, two billing accountsOne company block. The account badge appears on every row of that block, because it now disambiguates something real. This is the Mega Saver case and it is the reason the parent level exists.
One brand, two territoriesOnly the user's territory is listed and counted (D27). The header states the scope; it never implies the parent is that small.
One location, two contractsTwo rows under the same location name, each labelled by contract, each with its own cadence and total. invoice.mock.js already has this — Downtown Holdings holds three contracts — so it is real data, not a hypothetical. Merging them would invent an interval nobody sold.
Cadence changed mid-yearPlanned visits stand; projection recomputes from the new interval and anchor. The month it changed carries a marker so the gap in the rhythm is explained rather than reading as a bug.
Contract ends inside the horizonProjection stops. Columns after it are empty with a rule at the boundary and the row states Contract ends 31 Mar. Projecting past a contract invents work nobody sold.
A weekly locationFour to five visits in a month — the only case that overflows a cell. Renders 4 dates with a popover, so row height is fixed whatever the cadence.
Short months and month-stepping cadencesA cadence that divides into months steps in months, not milliseconds — otherwise “monthly” yields thirteen visits a year and the November DST change shifts every date after it. The 07 prototype already found this; the fix carries over verbatim.
Harmonize moves a visitThe cell changes immediately — one dataset. Projection re-anchors on the moved visit, so the rest of the row shifts with it, which is the honest consequence and the reason projections are drawn as estimates.
Missed visit still openAlert colour in its own month, and in the Period mode's overdue band, until it is rescheduled or cancelled.
Cancelled visitStruck through in its cell if it was planned. A projected date is never “cancelled” — it simply stops being projected.
Search matches a location, not a companyThe company block still frames the result, with non-matching locations collapsed behind +3 other locations. The hierarchy is never broken by a filter.
A location that moves company mid-yearIt appears under its current company for the whole row. Splitting a row across two blocks would double it; the history belongs on the location's own Schedule tab.
No company on the recordGrouped under No company on file rather than dropped — hiding demand is the failure this tab exists to prevent (07 D16).
TimezoneEvery date renders in the franchise timezone, as the grid does. Handoff §7 applies: the mock's non-visit times still carry a UTC skew.
Data

What the backend has to supply

All three reads are server-side. The twelve-month projection in particular must not be a client loop — it is the arithmetic a database does well and a browser does badly, and doing it on the client also puts the horizon boundary in the wrong place.

EndpointReturns
GET /companies?query=&page=The rail. Parent name, parentCompanyId, location count in territory, and the count of visits in the current period so the rail and the pane cannot disagree.
GET /visits/by-company?from&to&companyIdsPeriod mode. Companies with visits in the range, each with its locations and their visits — id, date, window, cadence, route, technician, status — plus header counts computed on the same filter as the rows.
GET /companies/schedule?months=12&companyIdsYear mode, for the whole selection rather than one company. Per location: address, account, contract, cadence, lastVisit, planned visits, projected dates, contractEndsAt, and scheduledHorizonEndsAt — the date the dashed rule is drawn on.

For the demo

Nearly everything is already in schedule.mock.js: the book is a cadence (VISIT_SITE_BOOK, 46 locations on 14–120 day intervals with two unscheduled) and isSiteDueOn / nextDueDayIndex already are the projection function. Three additions:

  • A parent company column on the book, many-to-one. Reuse the names billing already uses — sites.mock.js carries company and customerId, invoice.mock.js carries client and billTo — so the demo cross-links to invoicing instead of inventing a parallel customer list.
  • One brand deliberately split across two accounts and two territories, so D26 and D27 are demonstrable rather than described. This is the Mega Saver row.
  • A synthetic horizon — 21 days — so the straddling-month boundary is visible in the demo rather than theoretical.
Sequence

Build order

First
The tab, and Period mode on existing data. A companies tab config, the visits the week grid already holds grouped by company, the multi-select rail, the overdue band. Needs the parent field but no other backend change, and it alone answers “who are we visiting this week”.
Second
The matrix. Twelve columns, sticky label and header, cells, the planned/projected boundary, the /yr total, the location filter. This is the half that answers the actual objection, and the half that needs cadence and horizon.
Third
The edges. Deep links, CSV export, search across both levels, the multi-contract and contract-end cases, the weekly-cadence popover. Individually small; collectively the difference between a demo and a screen used on the phone to a customer.
One architectural note for whoever builds it Every existing scheduler tab is a variant of one FullCalendar resource timeline — ScheduleCalendarGrid → components/common/calendar → FullCalendar, and the Day/Week/Month switcher lives inside that common component. A companies entry in SCHEDULE_TAB_CONFIGS buys the tab button, the filter behaviour and a footer variant, but there is no seam for a non-calendar pane. The branch belongs at the ScheduleCalendarGrid call site in calendar/index.jsx, and this tab must skip the week/day/month fetch dispatch entirely — its scope control is not FullCalendar's view type.
Still open

Questions this design creates

  1. Where does the parent–child company relationship get stored, and who owns it? It does not exist in Edge today. Is it a new field on the customer record, a separate company table, or something Leads/SET already holds that Edge should read? The client asked for it to be “built”, which implies nobody has it.
  2. Can one location belong to two parents — a landlord and a managing agent — and if so which one owns the row?
  3. Is the displayed noun for an address Location, Property or Site? The definition is settled; the word is not. Recommendation: Location, since that is how the client speaks about it, set via tenant labels so Signal can keep Site.
  4. Is the contracted interval stored per location or per contract? The invoice model already gives a location several contracts. This design assumes per contract and draws a row each; confirm before the field is named.
  5. How far ahead does the scheduler actually generate visits? A fixed rolling window, a per-franchise setting, or as far as somebody has built? Decides where the dashed rule falls.
  6. What turns the tab on? The Visits tab's gate lets Signal through, and daily guarding has no cadence to project. A service-level flag — on for interval-based service, off for daily — rather than a tenant check, so a Signal client on a monthly patrol contract is not excluded by accident.
  7. Is CSV enough, or is a branded customer-facing PDF wanted? This grid gets read to customers; a PDF is a separate piece of work.
  8. Does Period mode need a “next 30 days” scope? Three scopes is what was asked for, but a franchisee planning filter stock may want it. Worth asking rather than adding on a hunch.
