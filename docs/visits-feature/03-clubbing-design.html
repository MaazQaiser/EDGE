FilterGo · Visits · Design spec, revision 2

Building one day's route out of whatever's outstanding

Not a weekday planner. A route builder for a single operator who is already on the road — pulling forward, catching up, and packing an eight-hour day as tightly as the driving allows.

Revision supersedes rev 1 Cross-day now core, not excluded Budget 8-hour man-day

The scenario that defines the feature

"On Tuesday the user wants to go to point A in the current runsheet, but he also merges yesterday's point B that he couldn't visit, and tomorrow's point C, into a single route."

Three different days, one of them already in progress, combined into one drive. Every decision below follows from taking this literally.

Revision

What changed, and why

Rev 1 locked same-weekday-only, because a hit's day is fixed in the code and I read the service cadence as contractually pinned to a day. That was wrong for this business: the frequency is contractual, the day is not. Filter replacement isn't a security patrol — nobody is auditing that Tuesday's visit happened on Tuesday.

Removing that constraint changes the shape of the whole thing. It stops being a screen you visit on Friday to plan next week, and becomes a tool you open on Tuesday morning with a van outside.

Screen

A budget you fill, not a list you tick

Because eight hours is the real constraint, the interface is built around a capacity meter rather than a count. Candidates are grouped by urgency — overdue first, then today, then what could be pulled forward — because that ordering is the actual decision the user makes.

Why the meter, and not a counter

A count of visits tells the user nothing about whether the day is achievable — six visits clustered in one town and six spread across a county are wildly different days. Showing service and travel as one filling bar makes the tradeoff physical: adding a distant visit visibly eats more of the day than a near one, before it's committed.

Live routes

Merging into a day already underway

This is the case that makes the feature worth building, and the one with the most constraints. When the target runsheet has started, three things change.

  • The route re-solves from where the van is, not from the depot. Optimising from the original start point would produce an order the driver has already invalidated.
  • Completed stops are frozen. They stay in the sequence as history and are excluded from reordering; only the remaining tail is optimised.
  • The budget counts elapsed time, not planned time. If four hours are already gone, the meter shows four hours consumed regardless of what was scheduled.

The in-progress stop — where the worker is right now — should also be frozen. Reordering around someone mid-service is how you produce a route that tells them to drive away from the site they're standing in.

Rules

What blocks, what warns

ConditionBehaviour
Route exceeds 8 hours Warn Overflow shown in amber on the meter with the specific visits that don't fit. Primary action offers to move them into a second route rather than dropping them.
Route ends after the runsheet's scheduled end Allow End time is pushed to match the computed finish. The new end is stated before applying, never silently.
Visit pulled forward from a later day Warn Labelled with its original day throughout. Confirmation offers to notify the client of the changed time.
Visit is overdue from a past day Allow Surfaced first and pre-sorted to the top. This is the case the tool exists for.
Moving the visit breaches the site's service interval Warn See the interval note below — the frequency is contractual even when the day isn't.
Visit already sits on another runsheet Allow Treated as a move. The source route is recomputed and its owner told. Both routes shown in the confirmation.
Target stop is completed or in progress Block Frozen in the sequence. Cannot be reordered or removed by this flow.
Visit is cancelled Block Never listed as a candidate.
Van isn't carrying the filters the selection needs Warn The existing supplies forecast already computes filter quantities for a set of visits. Show the load the selection implies before applying.
The risk hiding in "frequency is contractual, day is not" Both halves of that are fine on their own, but together they drift. If a site is contracted for a filter change every 30 days and each one gets pulled a few days either way, the gap between consecutive visits can quietly grow past 30 without any single move looking wrong. The guard is cheap: warn when a move would put this visit more than the contracted interval after the last completed one. Worth confirming the interval is actually stored per site.
Side effect worth designing properly You mentioned emailing the client when the visit time changes. That makes the confirmation step the only place in the product that sends customer-facing mail from a scheduling action — so it needs an explicit, checked-by-default control listing exactly which clients get contacted, not a silent background send.
Still open

Questions this revision creates

  1. Is the 8 hours fixed, or per worker? Part-time or contracted hours would make it a per-person setting rather than a constant.
  2. Is the service interval stored per site? Needed for the drift guard above. If it's only implied by the recurrence pattern, we can derive it, but less reliably.
  3. When a visit is pulled forward, does its original slot disappear or come back? D7 says the recurrence is untouched and only this occurrence moves — worth confirming that matches how you think about it.
  4. Who is the client notification addressed to, and do we already hold a contact for the site? There are external contacts in the codebase, but not obviously wired to visit scheduling.
  5. Should overdue visits ever expire? A visit missed six weeks ago that's still a candidate will clutter the list forever. A cutoff, or an explicit write-off action, is probably needed.
  6. Break rules — flagged open on your side. Once known, the meter is the natural place to show a break as a block in the bar.
  7. Day tolerance — you flagged this as needing exploration. If there's a "within ±N days is fine" rule, it should drive which pull-forward candidates are suggested rather than merely allowed.
