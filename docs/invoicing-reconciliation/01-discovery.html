Reconciliation & Outstanding

Giving the back office a straight answer to three questions: what have we billed, what actually came in, and where do those two disagree.

Status Second pass — UI refined, period view added Date 11 Aug 2026 Branch feature/scheduler-ui-refresh For Client review

Section 1

Where we started

An audit of the invoicing module as it exists today, before anything was designed.

The module already does a lot: it lists invoices, creates and edits them with line items pulled from payroll, pushes them to Sage or QuickBooks, previews and downloads the PDF, exports CSV, and merges contracts for combined billing. What it does not do is tell anyone whether the money arrived.

Three findings that shaped the design

Finding 1 — payment state was a single boolean

An invoice was either paid or not. Recording a payment meant marking it paid in full, for its exact grand total, by cash or cheque, and only for franchises on QuickBooks. No part payments, no payment history, no reversal, no record of how much actually arrived. You cannot derive an outstanding balance from a boolean — that single fact is why this feature needs a data model change and not just a new screen.

Finding 2 — "status" is not payment status

The Status column tracks the accounting-system sync: Pending → In Progress → Sync Approved → Sync Failed. It says nothing about money. So paid-vs-unpaid is a genuinely second, independent axis, and users will conflate the two unless the interface separates them deliberately. It also produces a discrepancy class nobody was tracking: an invoice that was raised but never pushed, and therefore can never be paid, because the customer has never seen it.

Finding 3 — there is already a button called "Invoice Reconciliation"

It sits in the invoices toolbar. It is a copy of the export modal that calls the payroll CSV endpoint and downloads a payroll file named invoices*.csv. It has never reconciled anything. Whatever we ship either replaces that button or has to live beside a control that already owns the word — worth a decision in the meeting.

Section 2

What "reconciliation" means here

The word covers at least three different products. This is the one we scoped.

The brief was insight into payments, and the discrepancies. That is not bank-statement matching and it is not an audit against Sage — it is a view that puts billed money and received money side by side and highlights every place they disagree. Eight situations qualify as a discrepancy. Five are visible from an invoice and its own payment history; three need reasoning across invoices and are deliberately out of the first draft.

Why 6–8 are deferred but the model is not

Those three are only expressible if a payment can exist independently of an invoice. So payments were built as records in their own right — a receipt has a customer, an amount, a date, a reference, and an optional invoice — even though the draft always fills the invoice in. That means unidentified receipts, duplicates and credit application can be added later without reworking anything. It costs nothing now and it is the difference between a view that can show five discrepancy types and one that can show eight.

Section 3

Decisions taken

Made to get a first draft on screen. Every one of them is reversible, and each names the reasoning so it can be argued with.

D1 · Scope

Reconciliation means payment insight and discrepancy detection — not bank-statement matching, not a Sage/QuickBooks audit.

Statement matching needs a payment ledger underneath it to match against, so it cannot come first. A system-drift audit only makes sense once we know which system owns payment truth, which is still open (Q1).

D2 · Placement

Outstanding is a second tab inside Invoices, not a new top-level page.

Same records, two different readings — a document list and a money position. Tabs keep both a click apart and reuse the existing filters, search and table. A new sidebar entry would split invoice work across two destinations.

D3 · Data model

Payments are first-class records with a nullable invoice link, not fields on the invoice.

A receipt can arrive that matches no invoice, matches the wrong one, or arrives twice. None of that is expressible if payment lives inside the invoice. Balance, paid state and discrepancy flags are all derived from the ledger, never stored twice.

D4 · Two axes, shown together

Payment state gets its own column, adjacent to the existing sync Status.

The pair is what tells the reader whether an unpaid invoice is the customer's problem or ours. Discrepancy chips sit in the same cell as the payment state, because they only ever qualify it — "paid, but late".

D5 · Aging

Measured from the due date, in bands: not yet due, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+.

The question is how late the money is, not how old the paperwork is. An invoice raised 90 days ago on 90-day terms is not a problem; one raised 20 days ago on 7-day terms is.

D6 · Payment capture is no longer QuickBooks-only

One "Payments" action on every issued invoice, replacing the QuickBooks-gated "Pay Now" popover.

Recording what a customer sent is bookkeeping every franchise does, whether or not their accounting integration can hear about it. It is disabled until the invoice has actually been issued — money cannot arrive against an invoice the customer has never seen. This one is a real behaviour change and worth confirming (Q3, Q10).

D7 · Amounts are editable; overpayment is allowed

The amount field defaults to the outstanding balance and can be changed, with a warning when it exceeds the balance.

Pre-filling keeps the common case one click while making a part payment a deliberate edit. Blocking overpayment would just push the discrepancy off-system, where it can't be seen.

D8 · Reversal, not editing

A mis-keyed receipt is reversed and re-entered rather than edited in place.

Ledgers that can be quietly rewritten cannot be trusted. Reversal keeps the history legible. Whether reversal needs its own permission and an audit trail is open (Q3).

D9 · Credit notes are their own state

A credit note reads as "Credit note", not "Overpaid".

Its negative balance means something different from a customer sending too much: it is money we owe them. Only a genuine surplus counts as an overpayment discrepancy.

D10 · Filters narrow the queue, never the totals

Clicking an aging band or a discrepancy chip filters the customer list below; the headline figures keep describing the whole book.

Otherwise the reader loses the number they were reasoning about the moment they drill into it.

D11 · Frontend only

The whole module now runs on a local mock domain — no backend involved.

31 invoices across 5 customers, dated relative to whenever the demo is opened, hand-tuned so every state on the list above appears on screen. Recording, reversing, approving and editing all persist for the session, so the flows can be demonstrated, not just described.

D12 · Two tabs, two questions

The All Invoices tab is always scoped to a period — this month by default — and answers “what happened”. The Outstanding tab is unscoped and answers “where do we stand today”. Neither tries to be both.

“As of now” and “between two dates” need different arithmetic and a different layout. Scoping the listing to a stated window is also what makes the numbers above it mean anything — an unscoped “billed” total is a lifetime figure nobody asks for.

D13 · The period overview is a permanent widget, not a drawer

Billed / received / still open / collected for the chosen period, then the period presets and range, then the filter pills that narrow the table. The drawer that used to hold this has been deleted.

The numbers describe the very rows on screen, so hiding them behind a button meant the table's own totals were only ever visible somewhere else. Always-on also makes the scoping honest: the table is always filtered to a window, and now the window is stated rather than implied.

D14 · Aging and exceptions are controls, not readouts

A 6px proportion bar for shape, then a row of buttons to filter by.

They were always filters wearing the costume of a readout, which is most of why the page felt like a dashboard.

D15 · The "unpaid, overdue" chip is gone from the tab

Aging says when, the Unpaid state says whether. A chip saying both is the same fact a third time. It survives as a pill in the period overview, which has no aging rail to say it instead.

A deliberate inconsistency between the two views, justified by context rather than by symmetry.

D16 · No reconciliation button at all

The mislabelled "Invoice Reconciliation" toolbar button is gone. The period overview above the table does its job, permanently and without being asked.

A button that opens a panel of numbers about the table you are already looking at is a button that should not exist. The old payroll-CSV modal is unwired; its file stays because the export modal shares its stylesheet.

D17 · Invoices count by raise date, money by arrival date

In the period view, receipts are split into "settling this period's invoices" and "settling earlier invoices".

Those two sets never line up, and the gap is the reconciliation. In the demo's July: $19,542.60 arrived, but only $691.20 of it belonged to July's billing. Summing them into one figure would hide the only insight the view has.

D18 · Credit notes are reported, not netted

The period view shows credits as their own figure rather than subtracting them from "billed".

Netting made "still open" larger than "billed", which reads as a bug even when the arithmetic is right.

D19 · The app's own idioms, not new ones

Three compositions were copied from elsewhere in the product rather than invented: the divided stat strip from the dashboard, inline filterable counts from the schedule stats footer, and the shared table left exactly as it is — 48px single-line rows, one field per cell, a nested table for the expansion.

Token-level compliance turned out not to be the same thing as looking like the product. An earlier pass used the correct Typography variants and Button variants and still looked foreign, because the compositions were mine: KPI cards where the app uses a divided strip, bordered filter buttons and chips where the app uses inline counts, and two-line table cells where every other table in the app is single-line. Column labels are Title Case to match the module.

Section 4

Two views, and why

The first attempt put everything on one screen and it read as a dashboard. This is the second pass.

The original draft stacked four equal-weight KPI cards, then a five-card aging panel, then a five-chip discrepancy panel — three panels of readout before a single actionable row appeared. Worse, two of those panels were secretly filters dressed as readouts. It looked like something to admire rather than something to work through.

The fix was almost entirely subtractive:

WasNowReason

Five aging cards with barsOne 6px proportion bar plus a row of filter buttonsIt was always a filter. Make it look like a control.
Five discrepancy chipsFour exception chips — "unpaid, overdue" removedAging says when; the Unpaid state says whether. A chip saying both was the same fact a third time.
Billed / Received / Balance / Attention columnsBalance dominant; billed and received moved into the expansionCollection needs one number per customer, not three.
Queue below three panelsQueue visible immediately, all on one cardIt is the work, not the footnote.
One screen for "now" and "a period"Two tabs: Outstanding for now, All Invoices scoped to a periodDifferent arithmetic, different layout. See D12.

The end-to-end path

  • 1 · Narrow — click "31–60 days late", or "Short paid", or neither.
  • 2 · Pick a customer — the queue is grouped by customer, sorted by balance, showing how late their oldest item is and what is odd about their account.
  • 3 · Expand — billed / received / credits held for that customer, then each open invoice with its balance ("of $5,378.40 · $4,033.80 received"), its state, and its exceptions.
  • 4 · Open or record — the invoice number opens the invoice itself; Record payment opens the ledger drawer.
  • 5 · Record — amount (pre-filled with the balance), method, date received, reference. Part payments and overpayments both allowed; each receipt reversible.
  • 6 · See it land — close the drawer and the headline has moved. Verified: recording $2,602.80 took the total from $112,017.60 to $109,414.80, dropped open invoices 21 → 20, and moved collection 35% → 37%.

View 2 · All Invoices — a period, always on

A permanent widget above the invoice table, not a drawer. Four figures for the chosen window, then the period control — presets (this month · last month · last 3 months · year to date) plus an explicit range — then the pills that filter the table beneath it. This month is selected by default, and the table is scoped to it from first paint.

The pills carry their own counts for the period, so you can see what a filter would leave before clicking it: 9 All · 6 Unpaid · 1 Part paid · 2 Paid · 1 Credit note, then the exceptions — 1 Short paid · 4 Unpaid, overdue. They narrow the table and never the figures above, and they reset when the period changes, because the overview only offers pills that have matches.

The number that makes this view worth having

Of the $19,542.60 that arrived in July, only $691.20 settled July's invoices — the other $18,851.40 settled earlier ones. Invoices are counted by the date they were raised and money by the date it arrived, so a single "received" figure would quietly conflate this month's cash with last month's billing. The drawer splits them, then lists every receipt with the invoice it settled, tagging the ones that reach back into an earlier period.

Below that: the discrepancies among invoices raised in the window, then every receipt (date, customer, invoice, method, reference, amount), then a CSV export of exactly that list.

View 3 · All Invoices tab — paid and unpaid at a glance

  • A Payment column: Unpaid · Part paid · Paid · Overpaid · Credit note, plus exception chips.
  • A Balance Due column, open balances emphasised, credits marked as such.
  • A payment-state filter beside the existing status and type filters.
  • CSV export extended with amount paid, balance due, payment state, days overdue and discrepancies.

Section 5

Open questions

Ordered by how much the answer changes. The first four change the data model or the permissions; the rest change screens.

  1. Q1 Who owns the truth about payments — Filter Go, or Sage / QuickBooks? Blocking Why it matters — If the accounting system owns it, this becomes a read-only mirror plus a drift check, and recording a payment in Filter Go is the wrong affordance entirely. If Filter Go owns it, we need to talk about pushing payments out to the accounting system. The draft assumes Filter Go can record payments, which is the current behaviour, but only for QuickBooks franchises.
  2. Q2 What does a payment actually look like when it reaches the back office? Remittance advice, a bank statement, a cheque in an envelope, a portal notification? Blocking Why it matters — This decides whether discrepancies 6–8 (unidentified receipts, duplicates, credit application) are real problems worth building, and whether statement import is a later phase or never. It also decides what the "reference" field should be labelled.
  3. Q3 Should recording or reversing a payment be a separate permission from editing an invoice? Blocking Why it matters — Today anyone who can update an invoice can declare that money arrived, and the draft inherits that. For most organisations, touching the money ledger is a different level of trust from fixing a line item. Related: does reversal need an audit trail of who and when?
  4. Q4 Are 30 / 60 / 90-day aging bands right for your terms, and is due-date aging the convention you use? Blocking Why it matters — Cheap to change now, awkward once people quote the numbers in meetings. Filter Go terms in the demo run 7 to 30 days, which makes 90+ a genuinely bad place to be — some businesses would rather see 7 / 14 / 30 / 60.
  5. Q5 Credit notes: should the user be able to apply an open credit against an outstanding invoice in-app? Soon The draft shows credits held as a headline figure but has no way to spend them. Also unresolved: the invoice list currently displays credit notes as $0.00 because negatives are clamped, while the Outstanding tab shows the true signed amount.
  6. Q6 Overpayment policy — hold as credit, refund, or refuse the entry? Soon The draft holds it as credit and warns at entry. A refund path implies money going out, which is a different flow with different approvals.
  7. Q7 When a customer part-pays, do you need to record what was promised and when? Soon A "short paid" flag says something is wrong but not what happens next. A promised amount and date would turn the discrepancy list into a genuine follow-up queue.
  8. Q8 Who chases? Does an outstanding invoice need an owner, a chase status, and a last-contacted date? Soon This is the difference between a report and a workflow. It also decides whether the customer row needs a "log a call" action.
  9. Q9 Do customers receive a statement of account, or only individual invoices? Soon Grouping by customer makes a per-customer statement PDF a small addition. Sending it is a bigger conversation.
  10. Q10 German franchises have push-to-accounting hidden entirely. Should they see Outstanding, and can they record payments? Soon The module already branches on this tenant. If their invoices are issued outside Filter Go, "never issued" as a discrepancy is meaningless for them and needs suppressing.
  11. Q11 The invoice list is now 17 columns wide. Drop the tax and line-item subtotals, or add a column picker? Soon Payment and Balance Due earned their place, but the table scrolls horizontally to reach them. Grand total plus balance due may be all the money most readers need in a list.
  12. Q12 Should the period overview also respect the site filter, or is franchise-wide the right scope for a period close? Soon Today the period view covers the whole franchise. A per-customer period statement is a small addition if that is how you actually reconcile.
  13. Q13 Are the four presets right — this month, last month, last 3 months, year to date — and does your billing period run to calendar months? Soon If reconciliation happens on a 4-week cycle or a fiscal calendar, the presets are wrong and the default should change with them.
  14. Q14 Should reminders go out automatically at fixed intervals, or is chasing always a human decision? Later Automated dunning changes the tone of the whole feature and needs email templates, per-customer opt-outs and a send log.
  15. Q15 Write-offs: who can declare a debt bad, and what does that leave behind? Later Without this, ancient unpaid invoices sit in 90+ forever and quietly poison every total on the page.
  16. Q16 Can one franchise bill in more than one currency? Later Everything above sums a single currency per franchise. Mixed currencies would mean the headline totals need either conversion or splitting.
  17. Q17 Is "the customer disputes this invoice" a state you need to record? Later Disputed invoices are overdue for a reason that isn't neglect, and mixing them into the chase queue makes the queue less trustworthy.
  18. Q18 Should the per-customer outstanding view also appear inside Site → Billing? Later There is already an orphaned per-site invoices tab in the codebase that nothing renders — the obvious home for it, sharing one component with the Outstanding tab.

Section 6

Deliberately not built

So nobody looks for it in the demo.

  • Bank statement or remittance import, and any automatic matching. Pending Q2.
  • Unidentified receipts, duplicate detection, credit application — the model supports them; no screens exist. Pending Q2, Q5.
  • Applying a credit note to an open invoice.
  • Chase workflow — owner, status, contact log, reminder emails. Pending Q8, Q14.
  • Statement of account per customer. Pending Q9.
  • Write-offs and disputes. Pending Q15, Q17.
  • Pushing recorded payments back to Sage / QuickBooks. Pending Q1.
  • A list of individual receipts for a period. It existed in the drawer that the period overview replaced, and no table of invoices can show it. If a cash view is wanted it should be its own tab, not a panel bolted to this one.
  • A shared filter bar across both tabs — the Outstanding tab filters itself through the aging and exception controls; the site and date filters apply only to the invoice list.
  • Site or customer scoping in the period overview. Pending Q12.
  • Comparing two periods side by side. The drawer answers one window at a time.
